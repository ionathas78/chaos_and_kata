# EARTH

**Root • Weight • Balance • Ground Force**

> **The phrase is the exercise. The stance is where its force becomes visible.**

Earth is rooted, decisive, structural, heavy, and ground-driven. Motion carries most of the training load. Every descent, rise, rotation, weight transfer, chamber, and recovery remains muscularly controlled.

Earth moves when it chooses to move, and where it arrives becomes structure.

- **FLOW** — continuous, actively loaded movement.
- **SNAP** — force resolves into a definite structure.
- **HOLD 3** — hold for 3 complete controlled breaths; establish and own the structure.
- **HOLD 5** — hold for 5 complete controlled breaths; deliberate isometric emphasis.
- **HOLD 7** — hold for 7 complete controlled breaths; reserved for positions where extended isometric loading specifically warrants it.

Breath notes govern transitions, FLOW sequences, and force issuance. HOLD counts already measure time in complete controlled breaths and need no separate breathing instruction. In general, inhale while gathering, opening, rising, or receiving; exhale through committed strikes, presses, descents, and force resolution. Never suspend the breath to create strength or complete a transition.

---

## 1. Root

**Breath:** Begin in Mountain Pose. Exhale into the fold. After the hold, inhale through the controlled unfolding and gathering action; exhale through the Double Palm Press and settling into Horse.

**Mountain Pose → Standing Forward Fold [3]**

Unfold under control. Gather the hands as the legs open.

**Double Palm Press SNAP → Horse [3]**

Do not relax during the rise or sink. The entire movement remains loaded.

## 2. Four Corners

**Breath:** Maintain continuous breathing throughout the circuit. Let short exhales accompany punches, elbows, and the final ward rather than assigning a separate full breath to every SNAP. Inhale naturally during withdrawals, receiving actions, and preparation for the back sweep; exhale through the controlled descent and sweep. Never suspend the breath during the low rotation.

From Horse, establish the first line.

### Corner A

Pak Sau / advancing control and entry →

**Right Punch SNAP → Left Punch SNAP**

The final left punch loads its own withdrawal.

### A → B

Sidestep + hip rotation →

**Left Horizontal Elbow SNAP**

The elbow creates the new facing.

### Corner B

Pak Sau / advancing control and entry →

**Punch SNAP → Punch SNAP**

The final strike loads the hips.

### B → C

Look back toward the new threat. Let the eyes lead the preliminary rotation through the shoulders and hips.

Shift the weight forward and descend under control. Let the hands receive the ground as the free leg extends.

**180° Hou Sao Tui / Back Sweep FLOW**

Drag the extended foot low through the rear semicircle while the hands control the descent and rotation. Keep the sweep deliberate rather than explosive.

At 180°, plant the sweeping foot. The new base resets the form's geometry and establishes the facing for Corner C.

Rise under control directly into the next engagement.

### Corner C

Pak Sau / advancing control and entry →

**Left Punch SNAP → Right Punch SNAP**

The reversed order leaves the right side prepared.

### C → D

Sidestep + hip rotation →

**Right Horizontal Elbow SNAP**

### Corner D

Pak Sau / advancing control and entry →

**Punch SNAP → Punch SNAP**

Withdraw the final strike into a circular receiving action.

Turn toward center as the arms open.

**Outward Ward SNAP → Horse [3]**

### Four Corners Pattern

**A → LEFT ELBOW → B → 180° BACK SWEEP → C → RIGHT ELBOW → D → ROOT**

This is one journey through four vectors, not the same combination repeated four times.

## 3. First Panther

**Breath:** Let the Horse release directly into the low transition. Keep the breath moving through Panther's compact attack, using short exhales for the low crescent kick and Leopard strike. Inhale naturally as the body retreats into the raised-leg Thai Check, exhale through the lead jab, then exhale through the same-leg Teep and inhale through its recoil into Crane.

Release the final Horse without standing fully out of the root. Transfer the weight and sink directly into:

**Panther FLOW**

Do not settle. Panther is a low, actively loaded structure in motion.

Attack directly from the compressed Panther structure:

**Low Inward Crescent Kick SNAP → Diagonal Leopard Strike SNAP**

As the imagined counterattack arrives, give ground and lift the lead leg into:

**Muay Thai / Thai Check FLOW**

Keep the checking leg airborne. From the one-legged defensive structure, issue a compact lead-hand interruption:

**Lead Jab SNAP**

Without touching the raised foot down, let the check become the chamber for:

**Same-Leg Thrust Kick / Teep SNAP**

Retract completely without touching down. Let the recoil itself become:

**Crane / Thai Check SNAP → HOLD 3**

Panther now attacks before yielding the range. The retreating Thai Check is the handoff into Muay Thai: the raised leg stays airborne through the jab, becomes the Teep chamber, and recoils directly into Crane.

This begins the first mirrored journey.

## 4. First Journey

**Breath:** From Crane [3], breathe naturally as the hip opens into Tree. After Tree [5], inhale as Tree closes and the body lengthens into Warrior III. After Warrior III [5], exhale gradually through the long controlled descent into Panther. Maintain continuous breathing through Panther → Plank and the ground sequence, with a stronger exhale for Knee-to-Elbow. Inhale through the lunge and rotational recovery as space permits. In the returning Panther, keep the breath moving through the Panther attack, inhale into the retreating Thai Check, exhale through the lead jab, then exhale through the Teep, then inhale through its recoil into the opposite Crane.

From Crane, open the hip and hands together:

**Tree → HOLD 5**

Without touching the free foot to the ground, smoothly close through Crane and tip the entire one-leg structure forward. The standing leg remains the root while the free leg extends behind and the arms lengthen with the developing hinge:

**Tree → Warrior III FLOW → HOLD 5**

There is no SNAP between Tree and Warrior III. It is one continuous balance transition.

From Warrior III, keep the same leg rooted and the other foot airborne. Bend deeply through the rooted leg while folding the airborne leg forward beneath the body. Continue lowering the center until that free foot finally returns to Earth as the low structure becomes:

**Panther FLOW**

There is no touchdown between Crane, Tree, Warrior III, and the descent into Panther. Panther is the first point at which the free foot returns to the floor.

Shift backward under control, lengthening the forward leg as the hands receive.

Reverse the transfer. Drive forward again and let the hands descend toward Earth. As Panther carries the body down, close the hands into vertical fists and let the knuckles progressively accept the load with the wrists straight.

**Knuckle Plank [5]**

Bring the knee of the working side forward:

**Knee → Same-Side Elbow [3]**

Return under control:

**Knuckle Plank [3]**

Draw that knee forward again and drive the foot through into a lunge. Rotate through the hips and torso as the lunge creates:

**Revolved Panther FLOW**

Unwind the rotation under control into neutral Panther geometry:

**Revolved Panther FLOW → Panther FLOW**

The reset Panther restores the compressed low structure. Attack from it:

**Low Inward Crescent Kick SNAP → Diagonal Leopard Strike SNAP**

Give ground into:

**Muay Thai / Thai Check FLOW**

Keep the checking leg airborne and issue:

**Lead Jab SNAP → Same-Leg Thrust Kick / Teep SNAP**

Recoil completely without touchdown. Capture the returning leg as:

**Opposite Crane / Thai Check SNAP → HOLD 3**

The first journey has now created the beginning of the second.

## 5. Second Journey

**Breath:** Mirror the first journey. Breathe naturally as Crane opens into Tree. After Tree [5], inhale into the Tree-to-Warrior III extension. After Warrior III [5], exhale gradually through Warrior III → Panther. Keep the breath moving through Panther → Plank and the ground work, with a stronger exhale on Knee-to-Elbow. Inhale through the lunge and recovery where comfortable. In the returning Panther, keep the breath moving through the Panther attack, inhale into the retreating Thai Check, exhale through the lead jab, then exhale through the Teep, and inhale through the final Crane FLOW as the landing establishes Horse.

From Opposite Crane, open the hip and hands together:

**Opposite Tree → HOLD 5**

Without touching down, smoothly close through Crane and tip forward on the same standing leg:

**Opposite Tree → Opposite Warrior III FLOW → HOLD 5**

From Warrior III, keep the standing leg as the root and the opposite foot airborne. Bend deeply through the root while folding the airborne leg forward beneath the body. Continue the controlled descent until the free foot finally returns to Earth in:

**Opposite Panther FLOW**

As on the first side, there is no touchdown from Crane through Tree, Warrior III, and the descent into Panther.

Shift backward under control as the hands receive.

Reverse the transfer and drive toward Earth. As Panther carries the body down, close the hands into vertical fists and let the knuckles progressively accept the load with the wrists straight.

**Knuckle Plank [5]**

Bring the working knee forward:

**Knee → Same-Side Elbow [3]**

Return:

**Knuckle Plank [3]**

Draw that knee forward again and drive the foot through into the lunge. Rotate through the hips and torso so the lunge becomes:

**Opposite Revolved Panther FLOW**

Unwind:

**Revolved Panther FLOW → Panther FLOW**

From the reset Panther, attack directly:

**Low Inward Crescent Kick SNAP → Diagonal Leopard Strike SNAP**

Give ground into:

**Muay Thai / Thai Check FLOW**

Keep the checking leg airborne:

**Lead Jab SNAP → Same-Leg Thrust Kick / Teep SNAP**

Recoil completely without touchdown:

**Crane / Thai Check FLOW**

This final Crane is a transition rather than another hold. Plant the gathered leg wide and let the landing establish:

**Horse [5]**

### Mirrored Journey Pattern

**PANTHER FLOW → LOW CRESCENT KICK → LEOPARD STRIKE → THAI CHECK → LEAD JAB → SAME-LEG TEEP → CRANE [3] → TREE [5] → WARRIOR III [5] → PANTHER FLOW → KNUCKLE PLANK [5] → KNEE [3] → KNUCKLE PLANK [3] → LUNGE → REVOLVED PANTHER FLOW → PANTHER FLOW → LOW CRESCENT KICK → LEOPARD STRIKE → THAI CHECK → LEAD JAB → SAME-LEG TEEP → OPPOSITE CRANE [3]**

**TREE [5] → WARRIOR III [5] → PANTHER FLOW → KNUCKLE PLANK [5] → KNEE [3] → KNUCKLE PLANK [3] → LUNGE → REVOLVED PANTHER FLOW → PANTHER FLOW → LOW CRESCENT KICK → LEOPARD STRIKE → THAI CHECK → LEAD JAB → SAME-LEG TEEP → CRANE FLOW → HORSE [5]**

> **Mirror journeys, not exercises.**

The Panther phrase is a transformation rather than a string of poses: Panther attacks from its compressed structure with a low crescent kick and Leopard strike, then yields into a raised-leg Thai Check. The checking leg remains airborne while the lead jab interrupts the advance, becomes the chamber for the same-leg Teep, and recoils into Crane. From Crane, the free foot remains airborne through Tree, Warrior III, and the controlled descent until Panther finally receives it.

Symmetry belongs to the complete journey. Each side travels through compression, upward force, distance creation, balance, extension, descent, ground contact, rotation, and recovery before the other side answers it or Earth returns to root.

## 6. Third Journey

**Breath:** Keep the rhythm martial and continuous. Inhale during chambering, stance reversals, and loaded recoil; use compact exhales through each kick and strike. Let the breath continue through Nami-gaeshi rather than bracing through the one-legged defense.

The Horse created at the end of the second journey is the loaded doorway into Earth's final striking journey. This journey is mirrored, but its character is very different from the long Crane journeys: compact, percussive, and rooted. Performed crisply, the reversal should read as a continuation of one attack sequence rather than a pause between paired halves.

From the existing **Horse [5]**, transfer the center onto one leg without rising out of the root. Chamber the free leg and maintain a compact Shotokan guard.

**Mae Geri / Front Kick SNAP**

Retract under control. Let the kicking foot land forward and create the front stance at the same instant the same-side hand issues:

**Oi Zuki / Lunge Punch SNAP**

The landing and punch are one action.

Recoil that hand while the hips rotate through the rooted stance and the opposite hand fires:

**Gyaku Zuki / Reverse Punch SNAP**

Use the recoil to reorganize into a low lateral base without standing up:

**Kiba-dachi / Horse FLOW**

Shift the root fully onto one leg and fold the other foot inward and upward to remove it from a low attack:

**Nami-gaeshi / Returning Wave FLOW**

As the lifted foot returns and rebuilds the Horse structure, let the body rotation carry the elbow through a hooking path:

**Hooking Elbow SNAP**

Continue the descending action into the low counter:

**Ground Punch SNAP → Horse FLOW**

Do not settle. Reverse the stance and facing under control so the opposite leg is prepared to answer:

**Reverse Stance FLOW**

Continue directly into the opposite-side attack:

**Opposite Mae Geri / Front Kick SNAP**

Land forward with the same-side hand:

**Opposite Oi Zuki / Lunge Punch SNAP**

Rotate through the hips and answer with the opposite hand:

**Opposite Gyaku Zuki / Reverse Punch SNAP**

Reorganize into the opposite lateral root:

**Opposite Kiba-dachi / Horse FLOW**

Remove the opposite leg from the low attack:

**Opposite Nami-gaeshi / Returning Wave FLOW**

Let the returning foot rebuild the root while the body carries the opposite elbow through its hook:

**Opposite Hooking Elbow SNAP**

Continue down into the counter:

**Opposite Ground Punch SNAP**

Let the counter resolve completely back into:

**Horse [5]**

The journey leaves Horse through a direct kick-and-punch attack, answers a low counterattack with Nami-gaeshi, hooks through the opening, strikes down, rebuilds the root, reverses without settling, and carries the exchange through the opposite side before returning to Horse.

### Third Journey Pattern

**HORSE [5] → KICK → PUNCH → REVERSE PUNCH → NAMI-GAESHI → ELBOW HOOK → GROUND PUNCH → HORSE FLOW → REVERSE STANCE → KICK → PUNCH → REVERSE PUNCH → NAMI-GAESHI → ELBOW HOOK → GROUND PUNCH → HORSE [5]**

---

## 7. Return to Root

**Breath:** Let Cloud Hands restore smooth, even breathing after the percussive Third Journey. Keep the breath continuous through the lateral transfers. Exhale through the closing fold. After the fold hold, inhale as the body unfolds into Sun Salutation (single pose), then exhale as the arms lower into Mountain Pose.

The **Horse [5]** created by the Third Journey is Earth's final strength statement. Do not add a second Horse hold.

Soften the final Horse directly into:

**Cloud Hands FLOW**

**Right → Center → Left → Center**

Let the weight transfer remain rooted but gradually reduce the force of the movement. Allow the final center to narrow the stance and gather toward Natural rather than launching another attack.

**Mountain Pose FLOW → Standing Forward Fold [3]**

The opening fold leads into Earth. The closing fold begins the return to the yogic current beneath the form.

Inhale as the body unfolds under control and the arms rise overhead into:

**Sun Salutation (single pose) FLOW**

Exhale as the arms lower and the body settles into:

**Mountain Pose**

Stillness.

## Governing Principles

**The phrase is the exercise.**

A stance is not an isolated pose. It is the structure created by the technique that enters it, the muscular engagement maintained while occupying it, and the force that initiates the technique leaving it.

**Motion is the default training state. Stillness must justify itself.**

A HOLD captures a loaded structure. It does not create the load. Panther structures are normally FLOW landmarks: low, committed loading geometries that redirect or launch the next technique rather than places to settle.

**Technique creates stance.**

Do not move into a stance and then perform a technique. The technique should create the structure it needs.

**End of A contains beginning of B.**

Transitions are part of the resistance work. Do not rubber-band between positions.

**Mirror journeys, not exercises.**

Bilateral work belongs inside coherent choreography rather than as a checklist of isolated left/right repetitions.

**HOLDs count breaths. FLOW coordinates breath. SNAP issues breath.**

HOLD durations are measured in complete controlled breaths. Breathing notes therefore focus on movement between held structures and on force issuance. Never suspend the breath to manufacture stability or power.

Repeated geometry does not necessarily mean repeated training.

Audit the complete phrase by:

**Entry → Loading → Force Direction → Active Musculature → Capture → Exit**
