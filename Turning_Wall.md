# THE TURNING WALL

**Bastard Sword Strength Kata**

> The phrase is the exercise. The blade remains easy; each transition creates the next action.

- **FLOW** — continuous, actively controlled sword movement.
- **SNAP** — reserved for long point, measured thrusts, and the terminal cleave.
- **HOLD** — brief structural ownership, never a hard joint stop.

The hands may pass behind the head during an overhead wheel, but they never stop, reverse, snap, or abruptly catch the trainer there. Breathe continuously; inhale through gathering and recovery, and exhale through thrusts and the terminal cleave.

## 1. Enter the Ground

Begin left foot forward, sword low on the right.

Advance with two abbreviated rising cuts:

**Rising forehand → rising backhand**

Continue forward with one complete rising forehand, allowing the sword to collect high before the face.

Keep the cuts easy and gradually enlarge their paths.

## 2. Open the Road

Pass forward with a rising backhand.

Let the sword continue over the crown and around the right shoulder. Pass diagonally right as the loop resolves into a descending backhand.

Continue around the opposite shoulder. Pass diagonally left as the returning loop becomes a descending forehand.

**Rising backhand → right loop / backhand → left loop / forehand**

## 3. Reach the Cloak

Step laterally left with a rising forehand.

Bring the following foot through with a rising backhand.

Collect the second cut high before the face. Turn toward center and settle into a wide, comfortable stance.

## 4. Hold the Near Perimeter

Keep the left foot planted as the axis. Walk the right foot around through a half-circle:

**Rising forehand → overhead descending backhand → horizontal forehand**

Collect high before the face and transfer the weight.

Keep the right foot planted. Walk the left foot around through the opposite half-circle:

**Rising backhand → overhead descending forehand → horizontal backhand**

Allow the planted foot to pivot naturally with the knee.

## 5. Shift the Wall

Let the final backhand rise around the shoulder into a broad lateral clearing cut.

Sidestep across the rear edge of the center while the sword maintains the perimeter.

Establish the left-foot axis again:

**Rising forehand → overhead descending backhand → horizontal forehand**

Transfer through high forward guard.

Establish the right-foot axis:

**Rising backhand → overhead descending forehand → horizontal backhand**

## 6. Front and Rear

Let the final horizontal cut rise naturally into high guard.

Pass forward behind one relaxed vertical or slightly diagonal chop. Allow it to settle into long point.

Extend one measured thrust.

Recover the pommel toward the right shoulder. Turn through a passing step, allowing the recovery to become an overhead loop and descending cut toward the rear.

Settle into long point and thrust again.

**Chop → point → thrust → shoulder → turn and chop → point → thrust**

Exhale through each thrust without locking the elbows.

## 7. The Narrow Gate

Recover from the rear thrust while turning toward the original front. Let the sword settle forward and slightly right.

Release the left hand from the pommel and place it around the middle third of the blade. Draw the hilt toward the right ribs while keeping the point forward.

Step diagonally left. Raise the sword between your hands into a compact left-side cover. Let the cover turn immediately into a thrust.

Recover only far enough to regain structure.

Shift diagonally right into the opposite cover. Turn the point through center and thrust again.

**Shorten → left cover / thrust → right cover / thrust**

Keep the blade-hand wrist straight. The hilt hand establishes the structure; the blade hand steers the point.

## 8. Reclaim the Measure

Withdraw the second thrust while stepping backward. Keep the point forward until the body has cleared close measure.

Guide the point outward toward the left.

Release the blade hand and return it to the pommel. Let the changing grip draw the sword into a rising backhand.

Continue over the crown. The rising cut becomes the descending hawk without interruption.

## 9. Hawk–Quarter–Rake

Step with the descending hawk.

Continue into a horizontal quarter cut while turning toward the open line.

Carry the point upward through a rising rake.

Let the rake pass over the crown and resolve into one controlled descending cleave as you return beside the center.

**Rising release → hawk → quarter → rake → cleave**

Exhale through the final cleave. Do not mirror the passage.

## 10. Restore the Ground

Let the cleave curve outward at the bottom.

Complete one slow enclosing circle around the protected side. Collect the sword high before the face.

Lower it into the original low, point-forward guard.

Hold for one quiet breath.

## Memory Line

**Enter → Open → Cross → Ward Left → Ward Right → Shift → Ward Again → Front and Rear → Shorten → Left Gate → Right Gate → Lengthen → Hawk → Quarter → Rake → Cleave → Restore**

## Practice Notes

- Use a synthetic bastard-sword trainer with sufficient clear space in every direction.
- Keep the grip easy through circles; firm it only briefly at point control.
- Let the hips and rib cage turn with the blade rather than wrenching the shoulders independently.
- Stop a pass when a shoulder begins to shrug, elbows flare unpredictably, wrists collapse, or the blade plane wanders.
- Sharp pain, catching, instability, sudden weakness, or numbness ends the session.
- Practice the revised sequence as one slow pass before restoring the two-pass conditioning structure.
