# FIRE

**Acceleration • Expansion • Rebound • Propagation**

> **One flame catches another. The end of every action feeds the next.**

**Status:** Working draft

Fire is expansive, accelerating, rebounding, consuming, and rising. It begins in compression, grows through actively controlled motion, resolves through sharp force, and uses recoil as the chamber for renewed attack.

Fire may travel, but travel is not its subject. Fire may circle, but yielding is not its subject. Fire may strike hard, but compression is not its subject. Its subject is **propagation**.

- **FLOW** — continuous, actively loaded movement. Fire FLOW frequently accelerates.
- **SNAP** — force resolves into a definite structure without locking the joint.
- **HOLD 3** — hold for 3 complete controlled breaths; establish and own the structure.
- **HOLD 5** — hold for 5 complete controlled breaths; deliberate isometric emphasis.
- **HOLD 7** — reserved for positions where extended loading specifically warrants it.

Fire normally uses fewer and shorter holds than Earth. Stillness represents compression before ignition, never decorative posing.

The governing sentence is:

**COMPRESS → ACCELERATE → SNAP → REBOUND → REIGNITE**

Inhale while gathering, coiling, receiving, or rising. Exhale through strikes, kicks, descents, and force resolution. Never retain the breath to manufacture strength. The Dragon Bellows are controlled ignition breaths, not maximal respiratory effort.

## 1. Banked Ember

**Breath:** Begin in Mountain Pose. Exhale into the forward fold. Inhale through the controlled unfolding and opening arms, then exhale as the body settles into Horse. The held structure contains three controlled Dragon Bellows breaths. Inhale through the nose into the abdomen and lower ribs. Exhale audibly through the mouth while gently firming the trunk. Keep the throat open and never bear down or retain the breath.

Begin in:

**Mountain Pose**

Exhale as the hips hinge into:

**Standing Forward Fold FLOW**

Inhale as the body unfolds under control and the arms rise forward and open overhead, stopping short of uncomfortable shoulder range.

Step the left foot outward and begin sinking. Exhale as the arms circle down and cross over the lower ribs, right forearm outside left. Complete the descent into a moderate Horse structure:

**Crossed Ember Guard → Horse HOLD 3 / Dragon Bellows**

### Dragon Bellows

1. **Gather** — inhale through the nose; expand the abdomen and lower ribs. Exhale audibly as the forearms press slightly outward against imagined resistance.
2. **Heat** — repeat without increasing strain. Keep the shoulders down and the face relaxed.
3. **Ignite** — inhale into the loaded structure. During the exhale, shift slightly left and coil the torso as the crossed hands settle toward the left ribs.

The third breath is already the load for Splitting Flare.

## 2. Splitting Flare

**Breath:** Inhale through the initial coil. Use compact exhales through the descending strikes. Allow the rebounds and advancing steps to draw the next breath naturally without suspending it.

Drive from the left foot and step forward with the right.

The right arm unwinds from the left ribs in a long rising arc, then falls diagonally through the forward line as the action creates:

**Right Splitting Palm SNAP → Right Bow FLOW**

The left hand rises beside the temple as an active guard. Do not lock the striking elbow.

Allow the right hand to rebound past the hip. Its fall rotates the torso and lifts the left shoulder.

Step through with the left foot. Release the loaded shoulder so the left arm rises and falls in its own diagonal splitting strike:

**Left Splitting Palm SNAP → Left Bow FLOW**

The right hand guards. Let the left strike rebound low as the right arm rises into a long backfist or hanging-palm line:

**Right Rising Backfist FLOW**

Do not pause between these actions. The phrase grows slightly faster with each release.

### Splitting Flare Pattern

**COIL → RIGHT SPLIT → REBOUND → LEFT SPLIT → REBOUND → RIGHT RISE**

## 3. Wheel of Sparks

**Breath:** Keep the breath continuous through the wheel. Let compact exhales accompany the three force resolutions rather than assigning one full breath to each action.

Let the rising right hand roll into a downward gwa-like clearing backfist while pivoting approximately forty-five degrees right:

**Right Gwa / Clearing Backfist SNAP**

The right strike's recoil chambers the left arm. Transfer forward and swing the left forearm across the middle line in a broad sow-like arc:

**Left Sow / Sweeping Forearm SNAP**

The right hand folds toward the ribs rather than hanging behind the body.

Step into the new line with the right foot. Release the right hand as a penetrating diagonal palm or knife-hand strike:

**Right Chop / Penetrating Palm SNAP → Right Bow FLOW**

The left hand remains active near the opponent's arm or shoulder line, guarding, clearing, or pulling.

The three actions are one wheel:

**RIGHT CLEARANCE → LEFT SWEEP → RIGHT PENETRATION**

---

## 4. Thrown Spark

**Breath:** Keep the breath moving throughout the orbit. Inhale naturally through the recoil and descending half of the circle. Begin exhaling as the body rises, and let the exhale sharpen through the kick. Let the retraction gather naturally and exhale again as the landing and hand strike resolve together.

Let the penetrating right palm recoil across the chest toward the left shoulder. Shift the weight onto the forward right leg just long enough to free the rear leg.

As the right forearm crosses the upper chest, fold the left forearm across the lower ribs or abdomen. Draw both elbows comfortably inward to create a compact control frame around the core:

**Compact Crossed Ember Guard FLOW**

Keep the arms close as the knees and hips soften. Incline the torso toward the left-front corner, then circle the ribs, shoulders, and head down across the forward line, through the right side, and back upward. The pelvis remains the hub while the breastbone traces the rim:

**Full Coiling Flame Evasion FLOW**

This is a complete torso orbit around the waist, not a blind change of facing. Let the pelvis, knees, and feet turn or adjust enough to keep the joints free; do not plant the feet and grind the circle from the lumbar spine. The head follows the spine, and the arms remain compact enough to connect and control the rotating torso.

For a reduced-amplitude practice, trace the same pathway with a shallower incline. Preserve the full circle and its continuous force path rather than cutting it into a left bend and right bend.

As the torso rises through the right side, let the same spiral draw the left knee toward the centerline. Do not finish the orbit and manufacture a separate chamber.

The left leg sweeps from the left outside line inward across the front of the body in a controlled forward crescent kick at knee, thigh, or waist height. Let the inside edge of the foot describe the arc without forcing the hip:

**Left Inward / Forward Crescent Kick SNAP**

The kick is the released flare of rotation already created by the recoiling palm and waist orbit. It does not begin a separate kicking phrase.

Retract the leg cleanly rather than allowing the arc to drop it. Plant it into the new line. The landing creates a Left Bow structure and releases the upper-body compression by throwing the right arm outward:

**Right Diagonal Backfist / Splitting Palm SNAP → Left Bow FLOW**

The landing and hand strike resolve together. Keep the eyes oriented generally forward through the orbit and reacquire the line before the kick reaches full extension.

### Thrown Spark Pattern

**RIGHT-PALM RECOIL → COMPACT GUARD → FULL WAIST ORBIT → RISE + LEFT CHAMBER → LEFT INWARD CRESCENT → RETRACT → LAND + RIGHT DIAGONAL STRIKE**

## 5. Catch and Consume

**Breath:** Inhale as the first strike opens into the receiving action. Exhale through the pull and palm. Breathe naturally through the elbow turn, then exhale through the low pull and rising strike.

Open the right striking hand into an outward parry. The left hand reaches beneath it, closes around an imagined wrist, and pulls toward the left hip.

Sink through the pull into a wide lateral structure. The right hand erupts along the opened line:

**High Capture + Pull FLOW → Right Palm SNAP**

The right palm folds immediately into a compact elbow as the range collapses. Let the elbow turn the entire body toward the rear line:

**Right Turning Elbow SNAP**

As the turn finishes, the right hand descends into a low capture or rake. Pull toward the right hip while the left hand rises through the center:

**Low Capture + Pull FLOW → Left Rising Palm SNAP**

This creates bilateral push-pull work without photocopying the first half:

**HIGH CAPTURE → PULL → PALM → TURNING ELBOW → LOW CAPTURE → RISING PALM**

## 6. Falling Cinder, Leaping Flame

**Breath:** Inhale as the rising hand continues over the crown. Exhale through the descending forearm and controlled lunge. Let the low sweep gather the next inhale. Exhale through the rising backhand and jump kick, breathe during the landing, and repeat the same pattern on the opposite side. Never retain the breath to power the jump.

The rising left hand continues over the crown and becomes a diagonal descending forearm or chop. Step deeply enough for the technique to create a controlled left Low Lunge:

**Left Descending Forearm SNAP → Left Low Lunge FLOW**

Sweep the right hand low across the knee line. Let that crossing action coil the waist for the opposite hand rather than remaining at the bottom:

**Right Low Sweep FLOW → Left Cross-Rising Backhand SNAP**

The rising left backhand and left hip become one turning column. Rise over the left leg as the right knee lifts into a brief Crane-like chamber:

**Right-Knee Crane / Left-Leg Support FLOW**

Compress slightly through the standing ankle, knee, and hip. Make a low vertical hop and snap the chambered right leg forward. The supporting foot needs only to leave the floor briefly:

**Right Jump Front Kick SNAP**

Retract the kick before landing. Place the right foot forward into Right Bow. As the weight accepts the landing, drive the right forearm diagonally downward and let the bow deepen into a right Low Lunge:

**Right Bow LAND → Right Descending Forearm SNAP → Right Low Lunge FLOW**

The landing strike is already the beginning of the compact mirror. Sweep the left hand low, then use the crossing action to load the opposite rising backhand:

**Left Low Sweep FLOW → Right Cross-Rising Backhand SNAP**

Rise over the right leg as the left knee lifts:

**Left-Knee Crane / Right-Leg Support FLOW**

Release the mirrored low jump kick, retract it cleanly, and land forward:

**Left Jump Front Kick SNAP → Left Bow LAND**

Let the landing drive the left forearm diagonally downward as the stance deepens:

**Left Descending Forearm SNAP → Left Low Lunge FLOW**

The two halves mirror a short movement phrase rather than two long blocks:

**LEFT DESCENT → RIGHT LOW SWEEP → LEFT RISE → RIGHT CRANE + JUMP KICK → RIGHT LANDING DESCENT**

**RIGHT DESCENT → LEFT LOW SWEEP → RIGHT RISE → LEFT CRANE + JUMP KICK → LEFT LANDING DESCENT**

For the grounded version, keep the supporting foot planted and perform the same chamber, front kick, retraction, landing, and descending forearm. Use the grounded version on a hard or uncertain surface, under fatigue, or whenever the knees, ankles, balance, or breath ask for it.

## 7. Tiger Takes the Flame

**Breath:** Keep the rhythm martial and continuous. Use short exhales through each swat and palm heel. Let the twin chamber gather an inhale, exhale through the pounce, and inhale naturally as the rake pulls toward the ribs.

Rise from the left lunge and turn left. Form a broad Tiger Claw and drive the swat from the waist, outward and slightly downward, as the body settles into Left Bow:

**Left Tiger-Claw Swat SNAP → Left Bow FLOW**

Let the swat's recoil turn the waist into a right palm-heel strike. Keep the fingers curved and alive rather than driving with the fingertips:

**Right Tiger-Palm SNAP**

Reverse through center and settle into Right Bow as the right claw clears the opposite line:

**Right Tiger-Claw Swat SNAP → Right Bow FLOW**

Rebound immediately into the answering palm heel:

**Left Tiger-Palm SNAP**

Draw both elbows sharply toward the ribs without shrugging. Keep both hands clawed:

**Twin Claw Chamber SNAP**

Drive both palm heels forward together while remaining in Right Bow. The right palm naturally reaches several inches beyond the left because the right hip and shoulder lead. Both palms arrive on the same beat, with elbows softly bent:

**Double Tiger-Claw Pounce SNAP → Right Bow FLOW**

Close the fingers against imagined resistance. Rake both claws down and slightly outward, then pull toward the ribs with the forearms and upper back:

**Twin Rake + Pull FLOW**

The phrase mirrors two compact pairs, then escalates from alternating claws to both claws together:

**LEFT SWAT → RIGHT TIGER-PALM**

**RIGHT SWAT → LEFT TIGER-PALM**

**TWIN CHAMBER → DOUBLE POUNCE → TWIN RAKE + PULL**

## 8. Retreating Reverse Wheel

**Breath:** Let the rake gather an inhale. Keep breathing as the wheel travels backward. Exhale through the final downward snap, then return to ordinary breathing as the stance narrows.

Do not stop after the Tiger pull. The pull shifts the weight away from the forward right foot and initiates a deliberate three-step withdrawal.

Release the claws as the **right foot retreats**. Let both hands drop beside the hips and begin sweeping backward:

**Right Retreating Step → Hands Drop and Sweep Back FLOW**

Let the **left foot retreat** as both arms travel backward, outward, and rise along the sides. The feet carry the body, the waist drives the shoulders, and the arms complete the wheel:

**Left Retreating Step → Reverse Double-Arm Wheel FLOW**

Take a third **right retreating step**, opening the foot into a shallow Horse as the arms crest over the upper line. Do not pause with the hands behind the head:

**Right Retreating Step → Shallow Horse FLOW**

As the stance settles evenly, bring both arms down outside the body in a forceful two-armed splitting snap:

**Twin Downward Splitting Palms SNAP**

The wheel is traveling defensive work rather than an in-place flourish. It withdraws from the close range created by the Tiger pounce, clears the pursuit line, and rebuilds a stable guard at distance. If the form calls for an in-place evasion instead, use the Coiling Flame Evasion rather than removing the wheel's footwork.

Straighten gradually and draw the left foot in:

**Mountain Pose FLOW**

Inhale through an Upward Salute, then exhale from the hips into:

**Standing Forward Fold FLOW**

From the fold, complete one **Sun Salutation A** in your established expression:

**Half Lift → Plank → Chaturanga → Upward-Facing Dog → Downward-Facing Dog → Step Forward → Half Lift → Forward Fold → Upward Salute → Mountain Pose**

Let the transitions remain continuous. The salutation receives the heat of the form and carries it through one final rising cycle.

**Mountain Pose**

Remain still for two ordinary, unforced breaths.

The fire is contained, not extinguished.

# Performance Rhythm

Perform the complete form as one continuous journey. During early practice, two passes provide the intended session structure:

1. **Kindling Pass** — moderate range and approximately half speed. Establish the force paths. Make every recovery visibly chamber the next action.
2. **Burning Pass** — larger controlled arcs and stronger leg drive. Allow combinations to accelerate while preserving balance and deceleration.

Approximate duration for two deliberate passes: **12–15 minutes**.

Progress through:

- deeper actively controlled positions;
- stronger muscular intent;
- larger safe arcs;
- cleaner force transfer;
- sharper rebounds;
- smoother propagation;
- more precise SNAP endpoints;
- greater control under fatigue.

Do not progress primarily by adding repetitions.

The arms remain relaxed enough to accelerate but never limp. Elbows remain softly unlocked. The shoulders stay connected to the back. Kicks remain below the height at which balance, alignment, or retraction deteriorates.

Because breath retention can raise blood pressure, keep the breath moving throughout the form. Dragon Bellows should be audible and intentional but never maximal. Stop the breathing sequence if it produces dizziness, unusual head pressure, visual disturbance, chest discomfort, or unusual shortness of breath.

# Source Grammar

The form is synthetic rather than a reproduction of any inherited curriculum:

- **Piguaquan / Piguazhang** supplies long-range whipping power, alternating rise and fall, splitting actions, and rebound chambers.
- **Choy Li Fut** supplies the circular combination grammar, waist-driven links, and gwa–sow–chop family.
- **Taekwondo** supplies clean kicking chambers, extension, retraction, and crisp lower-body punctuation.
- **Capoeira** supplies selective turning and recovery logic, especially the principle that landing or recovery already begins the next action.
- **White Crane** supplies the brief one-leg chamber, opening and closing accents, and sudden close-to-open acceleration.
- **Eagle Claw** supplies consuming captures, rakes, and pulls that keep the non-striking hand active.
- **Tiger-style mechanics** supply the closing swats, palm heels, two-handed pounce, and heavy raking pull: rooted clearing actions escalate into sudden forward consumption and forceful gathering through the forearms and upper back.
- **Changquan** informs spatial composition, rhythm contrast, extended lines, and legible changes of facing.

The governing synthesis is:

> **Build Fire from Pigua's whip, Choy Li Fut's chain, Taekwondo's spark, and Capoeira's recovery. Let Changquan compose the stage, Crane shape a few bright edges, Eagle give the flame something to catch, and Tiger teach it to consume.**

# Governing Principles

**The phrase is the exercise.**

Every arc earns its amplitude through force generation, defense, reach, loading, recovery, or transition.

**Fire propagates.**

The previous technique's recoil creates the next technique's chamber. No reset is neutral.

**Fire mirrors in short phrases.**

Maintain bilateral training balance through paired right-left phrases built from a small number of tightly linked actions. Fire may kick, sweep, and rise on one side, then answer with the same short sequence on the other. The sequence should be large enough to create a coherent burst of movement, but brief enough that its mirror feels like a quick answering flare.

Do not build a long, developed passage on one side and then repeat that entire passage on the other after substantial separation. That expresses symmetry in blocks and gives the form a more deliberate, inexorable intention. When a mirrored phrase begins growing into a sustained section, end the mirror and let its recoil ignite something new.

Fire's unit of symmetry is the short sequence rather than the single technique or the extended section. It keeps the workout balanced through frequent, compact mirrored phrases.

**Arrival loads departure.**

The endpoint of every technique should chamber, preload, orient, balance, or position the body for what follows.

**The non-striking hand tends the fire.**

It guards, pulls, clears, captures, counterbalances, or chambers. It never decorates.

**Kicks are thrown sparks.**

A kick emerges from rotation or loading already present in the phrase. Its retraction and landing continue the same sentence.

**Stillness is combustion under pressure.**

Fire uses few holds. When it becomes still, the structure is visibly loaded and prepared to ignite.

**Amplitude remains controlled.**

Relaxed does not mean limp. Avoid ballistic end-range traction, hard elbow locking, blind high-speed turns, and kicks that cannot retract cleanly.

**Fire must still read as Fire when performed slowly.**

Elemental identity comes from the movement's mechanics: compression, acceleration, expansion, rebound, and propagation. It does not come from ornamental flame hands.

Absolutely no martial jazz fire.
